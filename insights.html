Deep analysis

The statistical work behind the recommendations: how strongly Steam player counts and Twitch viewership actually correlate, what a Random Forest model says drives viewership, and whether the scoring logic used elsewhere on this site actually predicts which games are about to rise. Generated straight from EDA.py — the latest month by default, with every earlier month and the all-time totals one dropdown away. Click any game (a point, a bar, or a stacked segment/legend entry) to highlight that game across every chart on this page; click it again, or the chip that appears, to clear it.

New here? Start with these 4 charts

You don’t need to read every chart. If you’re a new streamer deciding what to play and when to go live, go through these in order — each button jumps straight to the chart.

  1. What should I play? Green bars are games with strong viewers and players right now. Pick from the top, but skip anything far above the rest if you’re small — giants are hard to get seen in.
  2. Which day is least crowded? A tall bar means many viewers for few streamers — that’s the day a small channel is most likely to be noticed.
  3. What time should I go live? The peak is when the most people are watching; the hours just before the peak are often a good compromise. Set Timezone to “Local time-of-day” and pick your audience’s Region first.
  4. What’s realistic? Most streams are tiny and a handful are huge. Read the median (not the average) as your normal expectation — this chart keeps you from comparing yourself with the giants.

Then, when you’re comfortable: shows who you’d compete with at the busiest hour; checks whether a game’s player count predicts its viewers; and are the statistics behind the scoring — safe to skip at first.

Tip: click any game (a dot, a bar or a legend entry) to follow it across every chart. Hover or tap any bar for exact numbers.

Week and Day only apply within a specific month — choose "All years" + "All months" for the all-time view, or a specific month to narrow down. Weeks are calendar weeks within that month (Week 1 = days 1–7, and so on). Leave Week on "All weeks" and the Day dropdown lists every day of the month; pick a Week and Day narrows to just that week's days. Some charts (like the feature-importance model) need more data than a single day gives and won't show for that granularity — try Week or the whole Month instead.

Timing signals

Streamer activity, viewer engagement, game market share and the viewer-per-streamer ratio, all broken down by day/hour — filterable by clock reference and by broadcast region so you can see whether the pattern holds for a specific audience, not just the global average. Two of these charts also have a view toggle for a second dimension (which games make up the totals) alongside the simple view.

Region is estimated from each stream's broadcast language, not verified viewer location — treat it as a rough proxy, not a precise geography.
